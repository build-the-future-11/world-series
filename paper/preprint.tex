\section{Abstract}\label{abstract}

We study whether implemented structure produces measurable benefits
after accounting for verification, strong controls, and failure cases.
Nine related software modules cover optimization, adaptive memory,
symmetry-guided search, program synthesis, word-algebra coordinates,
constrained graph operators, weak dynamical discovery, conditional graph
computation, and tool routing. Their scientific questions remain
separate. We combine mechanism checks with bounded development
experiments and retain failed runs. Several apparent advantages
disappear under stronger controls: the learned optimizer has worse
loss-area performance than tuned fixed optimizers; expert lifecycle does
not outperform a simple stream predictor; learned symmetry proposals
reduce expansions but increase elapsed time; and conditional graph
routing is slower and less accurate than full refinement. A
graph-operator constraint study exhibits a scoped error reduction, with
adverse conditions retained. These results identify concrete mechanisms,
computational tradeoffs, and unfinished tests rather than establish a
universal architecture. The evidence is internally audited and includes
exact same-host replays, but is not independently confirmed.

\section{1. Introduction}\label{introduction}

A working implementation and a favorable benchmark number answer
different questions. Structured algorithms may satisfy algebraic
contracts yet add computational overhead, preserve valid paths without
reducing search, or exploit a task construction that favors a particular
representation. We investigate these distinctions in a shared research
workbench. The central methodological question is whether a claimed
mechanism survives a comparison that holds information access and
evaluation rules fixed while charging the work needed to use it.

This report retains the full development record rather than selecting
only successful modules. We do not pool incomparable endpoints or count
random seeds as new datasets. Each module has its own model, domain,
controls and limitations. The present contribution is a documented
computational investigation; a claim of methodological novelty requires
further comparison to the closest primary literature. This manuscript is
a development report, not a statement that all proposed architectures or
publication gates are complete.

\section{Related work and
positioning}\label{related-work-and-positioning}

Learning an update rule is established prior work: Andrychowicz et
al.~(2016) train recurrent optimizers through optimization trajectories.
QLearn's bounded controller is evaluated as a particular implementation,
not as the invention of learned optimization. Celo2 (Moudgil et al.,
2026) addresses learned-optimizer generalization and practical
optimization; its released pretrained model supplies an external
comparator here, with unmatched pretraining and unresolved data-overlap
limitations.

Sparse identification of dynamics likewise predates this work. Brunton,
Proctor and Kutz (2016) formulate discovery through sparse regression
over candidate nonlinear terms. Messenger and Bortz (2020) develop
weak-form identification to avoid pointwise derivative estimates and
reduce sensitivity to noisy observations. WPINN's sequential
observation-field and structured sparse-fit experiment belongs in that
established context. Its contribution must be assessed through the
additional structure and discriminating controls, not by renaming weak
regression.

These citations establish background and attribution. They do not
complete the closest-work review for all nine modules or establish
novelty for this collection.

\section{2. Experimental organization}\label{experimental-organization}

All studies use bounded local CPU reference paths. Configurations,
source identities, raw outputs and failures are retained in immutable
campaign directories. Selection data choose hyperparameters or
checkpoints; held observations are used for reporting. Tasks inspected
during development are not subsequently described as secret holdouts.
Most studies use eight seeds, but the independent population unit
remains the dataset or generator family. Seed-level variability does not
establish broad generalization.

We distinguish exact finite-domain contracts, numerical regression
checks, development comparisons, and independent replication. A
certificate establishes only its explicit domain and assumptions. A
successful program exit does not establish the scientific hypothesis.
Failed required cells remain in denominators or make an aggregate
unavailable; survivor-only means are not substituted. Exploratory
interventions on a failed model are labeled retrospective rather than
treated as new primary outcomes.

\section{3. Mechanisms}\label{mechanisms}

\subsection{3.1 Learned optimization and adaptive
memory}\label{learned-optimization-and-adaptive-memory}

QLearn produces bounded context-dependent updates and compares them with
tuned SGD, momentum, AdamW, architecture ablations and the official
pretrained Celo2 implementation. Its natural-data study uses binary
digit-pair tasks, with distinct pairs for controller training, selection
and held adaptation. The one dataset and unmatched external pretraining
limit the interpretation. MALIS separately implements discrete action
selection and rollback; its results are not evidence for the continuous
controller.

QAPEN combines frozen learned quantization, continuous residuals,
persistent expert identities, prospective activation, bounded replay,
and delayed memory utility. Predictions precede observations. Checkpoint
tests restore expert and optimizer state and reproduce continuation
predictions. Single-expert memory and rolling predictors isolate whether
retrieval or lifecycle management supplies any benefit.

\subsection{3.2 Verified search and program
synthesis}\label{verified-search-and-program-synthesis}

QWIPII searches finite token rings with rotations and adjacent swaps. A
proposed map is eligible for fixed-goal pruning only after exhaustive
verification of a complete multiset domain, bijectivity, preservation of
the goal and every unit-cost transition. A map that transforms both
board and goal can establish equivalence between problems without
preserving the goal of a single search. Empty or incomplete samples
cannot establish a certificate.

Serialized mappings are revalidated before use. Generator orbits are
closed and search stores concrete representatives, so returned actions
replay in the original coordinates. A trained ridge ranker predicts
single-generator orbit reduction from public goal and sampled action
features. Ranking does not authorize pruning; rejected proposals consume
the verification budget. A separate greedy state ranker never merges
approximate equivalence classes and does not guarantee shortest paths.

WCODE ranks a finite, typed program grammar using trained structural,
token and task-conditioned controls. Candidate enumeration and available
examples are held fixed. The benchmark measures verified completion in a
finite DSL, not general software engineering. Shared semantic functions
with earlier routing studies are explicitly disclosed.

\subsection{3.3 Algebra, graph operators and physical
discovery}\label{algebra-graph-operators-and-physical-discovery}

WFIM uses word concatenation with degree truncation. Positive diagonal
coordinates transport multiplication by applying the coordinate map,
multiplying, and applying its inverse. Multiplicative per-letter scales
cancel exactly; independent bounded per-word scales need not. Both
products remain isomorphic to concatenation. An unrestricted decoder may
absorb fixed diagonal feature reweighting, so downstream benefit must be
established under stated optimization, regularization and storage
constraints. A downstream coordinate study compares this mechanism to
the identical fixed algebra and to vector, bilinear and binding
controls.

WFT fits independent fine and coarse graph fields with equivariance and
coarsening constraints. The experiment uses explicitly verified
equitable coarsening and distinguishes valid reflection constraints from
deliberately invalid shifts. WPINN fits an observation-only neural field
or spline and then performs sparse weak-form identification, with
physical-structure constraints and derivative/weak baselines. Sequential
fitting is part of the original first scope; joint physics training is
optional and PDE extensions are deferred.

CWLNN selects nodes before expensive refinement. Router, local, coarse
and refinement computations are measured rather than masking already
computed outputs. Full refinement, flat networks and dense attention
provide controls. Ultron routes among bounded capabilities and verifies
outputs; the existing DSL results do not establish heterogeneous
scientific orchestration or independent verification.

\section{4. Results}\label{results}

\subsection{4.1 Optimization and memory}\label{optimization-and-memory}

At 32 adaptation steps, mean normalized loss area is 13.2809 for the
full learned controller, 11.9562 for SGD, 7.9893 for momentum, 6.4145
for AdamW and 7.7454 for official pretrained Celo2; lower is better.
These data do not support optimizer superiority. Wider tuning and longer
meta-training retain failures and divergence instead of supplying
complete favorable aggregates. Same-host replay of 400 original
meta-training trials matches saved states and failure status.

The causal memory study contains 512 stream-method cells and 163,840
predictions. Mean negative log likelihood is 0.61727 for the candidate,
0.61225 for single-expert memory and 0.55893 for rolling ridge.
Mechanism activation and checkpoint recovery therefore do not establish
incremental lifecycle benefit. Broader delayed-cue, noise and
cost-matched requirements remain open.

\subsection{4.2 Search and synthesis}\label{search-and-synthesis}

The learned QWIPII study uses five training goals of size three or four,
three selection goals of size five, and four held goal patterns of size
six. Eight seeds and four starts per held goal produce 128 cells per
method and 896 total paths. All paths replay. Learned ordering averages
21.3047 expansions and 16.9859 ms per query, compared with 23.9453
expansions and 9.0092 ms for random ordering. Brute search averages
25.1719 expansions and 1.2703 ms. Query times include discovery and
verification where used; training and selection costs are saved
separately.

All non-greedy methods match brute shortest-path lengths. Greedy ranking
returns longer paths in 16 of 128 cases. Removing the goal feature
increases learned-search expansions; removing the sampled transition
feature leaves the reported average unchanged. These are descriptive
results within four fixed ring patterns, not new puzzle families. One
timing observation per cell does not establish a hardware-general
efficiency claim.

WCODE evaluates 331 canonical finite functions with 28,416 audited
searches. Relational scoring at candidate cap 24 completes 10.54 percent
of held-family cases, compared with 9.80 percent for token scoring.
Held-composition completion is zero. Fifteen functions overlap older
WCODE/Ultron data, including four current evaluation families. The
evidence does not establish robust structural-ranking superiority.

\subsection{4.3 Graph operators, dynamics and conditional
computation}\label{graph-operators-dynamics-and-conditional-computation}

WFT contains 160 learned fits, 64 fixed or oracle controls and 2,688
transfer rows. Both constraints yield mean error 0.000303878 versus
0.000928561 without either constraint within the declared generator.
Both constraints are worse in 12 of 48 conditions. This is a bounded
benefit, not a claim over arbitrary graph distributions. Numerical
verification at nearly repeated spectral boundaries requires a disclosed
gap-sensitive tolerance.

WPINN contains 120 condition cells, twelve methods, 240 learned
observation fields and 6,720 replayed rollouts. Primary aggregates
remain incomplete when required integrations fail: unconstrained neural
weak fitting has 21 failed cells and the passive variant has three.
Alternative stiff solvers complete four passive held trajectories but
retain substantial prediction error. All 17 original failed polynomial
rollouts remain preserved. Retrospective support interventions do not
prove an exclusive failure cause.

CWLNN trains 128 models and reports 23,040 held predictions and 115,200
timing repeats. At half routing, learned routing has mean squared error
0.011939 versus 0.011412 for full hierarchy on the aligned task, and
0.032721 versus 0.029831 on diffusion. It is slower in both comparisons.
Flat depth-five processing is strongest on the diffusion condition.
Permutation audits agree to approximately 4.45e-16, supporting
implementation consistency rather than routing benefit.

\subsection{4.4 Learned word-algebra
coordinates}\label{learned-word-algebra-coordinates}

The WFIM study trains 80 models across eight seeds, two fixed tasks and
five encoders. All 40,640 saved predictions replay exactly after
checkpoint reload. On ordered-pattern frequency, learned coordinates
have held-template MSE 0.036352 versus 0.035816 for identity
coordinates, and held-length MSE 0.009514 versus 0.006033. Mean training
time is 9.75 versus 3.19 seconds. Thus the learned coordinates do not
improve the primary task over the identical algebra. The
order-insensitive count control is mixed. Encoders share a one-MiB
payload cap and the same decoder/output dimensions, but use unequal
bytes; this is not an exactly byte-matched capacity comparison.

\section{5. Discussion}\label{discussion}

Three recurring distinctions explain the outcomes. First, algebraic
validity does not imply useful parameterization: per-letter transport
cancels, and per-word transport remains an isomorphism. Second, less
nominal work does not imply less elapsed work: exact proposal
verification can dominate saved search expansions, and conditional
routing can cost more than full refinement. Third, a mechanism-specific
improvement does not establish the full architecture: retrieval can help
while expert lifecycle loses to a simpler control.

These observations motivate finite branch decisions. The implemented
per-letter product-expressivity claim is unsupported. Learned routing
benefit is unsupported in the measured graph study. Learned proposal
runtime benefit is unsupported in the ring study. The constrained
graph-field result remains a scoped empirical benefit requiring broader
comparisons and novelty review. None of these dispositions is converted
into independent confirmation.

\section{6. Limitations}\label{limitations}

The evidence includes small synthetic distributions, a single natural
digit dataset, repeated seeds within fixed families, incomplete
failure-affected aggregates and several unmatched total-cost components.
Current finite rings do not cover whole new puzzle families. WFIM
requires stronger byte-matched controls and full selection replay. WPINN
still needs held physical parameter regimes and inverse-PINN/data-only
comparators. CWLNN arbitrary-tree receptive-field matching remains
unresolved. Heterogeneous Ultron capability evaluation and independent
external verification remain unfinished. These gaps prevent a
submission-ready verdict for the full proposal collection.

\section{7. Reproducibility}\label{reproducibility}

The repository retains campaign protocols, source hashes, selected
checkpoints, predictions, failures and analysis receipts. The delivery
builder includes campaigns and Git history and verifies archived
contents before extraction. Fresh-environment checks have passed
installation, tests and selected artifact audits after portability
fixes. A further CWLNN fresh-environment audit was interrupted and
remains incomplete. Same-host exact replays are useful diagnostics but
do not establish independent reproduction. A final archive must include
this manuscript and the final completed analyses before release.

\section{8. Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

The workbench establishes several functioning mechanisms and documents
where their claimed advantages fail under stronger controls or explicit
cost accounting. Its present results justify scoped negative findings
and further discriminating experiments. They do not establish a unified
superior model, nine novel methods, or conference readiness. A useful
final publication must make a coherent, evidence-supported contribution
and preserve these limits.
